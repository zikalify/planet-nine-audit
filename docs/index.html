Planet Nine: current evidence map

Where a published orbit model says an unseen planet could be tonight, minus what published telescope searches already ruled out — plus the current status of the evidence that started the hunt.

This is not a detection. No observed position of Planet Nine exists. The map shows potential locations under one published model (Brown & Batygin 2021) propagated to the current date in your browser, combined with published search exclusions. It is model-conditional, not a measured probability.

How to read this map

  • Warm density: where the 21,740 model samples not flagged as detectable by the published ZTF, DES and Pan-STARRS searches would sit on the sky on the selected date. The samples come from the CaltechDATA v3 reference population (Brown & Batygin 2021; elements at epoch 1 June 2018, checksum-verified), propagated here with two-body orbital mechanics validated against JPL Horizons (see validation notes in the repository).
  • Cool overlay: the 78,260 model samples the published searches say they would have found. "Excluded" means excluded within this 2021 model, under each search team's own published assumptions.
  • Markers: published far-infrared candidate positions (IRAS/AKARI). All are unconfirmed; each note gives the paper's own caveats.
  • The concentration you see is mostly the model's orbital geometry (perihelion direction and inclination family), not new evidence.

What this is not

  • Not a detection, and not a live telescope feed.
  • Not a 2026 posterior. No public post-2021 orbit ensemble was found in our review; the newest published result (May 2026) says the clustering evidence behind the model is weaker than previously reported and its direction spans about half the sky (see below).
  • Not a claim that the planet is in the densest pixel. The 2021 model's sky map is exactly what the 2026 reassessment calls too narrow.

The dynamical evidence today (gravitational effect on other bodies)

The original case for Planet Nine is the apparent clustering of distant TNO orbits. We independently implemented the conditional-likelihood method of Siraj, Chyba & Tremaine (2026, ApJ 1003 76) and reconstructed their sample from published tables and JPL data. Our implementation reproduces every published value in their Table 1 (see validation column; tolerances ±0.15σ / ±3°):
